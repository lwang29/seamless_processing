# Seamless Interaction annotations: progress update (30 Sep 2026)

**Summary.** The pipeline now annotates the whole dataset and filters nothing out.
The first full build is done. Every recording in Meta's release has a row in the
tables, and so does every 30-second clip of every recording, about a million clips.
Each row describes the clip: posture, how expressive the person is, how they move,
who is speaking, audio and video quality, and the conversation the clip belongs to.
Each value also says how confident it is. To build a training set you write a filter
over the tables. Deciding what counts as good data is left to whoever trains the
model.

**What I'd like from you:** a look at the judgment calls in §3, any changes or
additions you'd like, and your view on the two questions in §5.

A note on vendors: Meta recorded the dataset through four vendors, V00–V03. Each
vendor has its own studio, cameras and body tracking, so some results below are
reported per vendor.

## 1. What's in it

The tables follow the structure of the dataset, from people down to 30-second
clips:

| table | rows | one row is |
|---|---|---|
| participants | 4,307 | a person |
| sessions | 5,102 | one recording session of a pair of people (several conversations) |
| prompts | 1,312 | a conversation scenario Meta gave the pair |
| conversations | 64,751 | one conversation between two partners |
| conversation segments | 515,749 | both partners over the same 30 seconds |
| recordings | 129,370 | one person's video, audio and body tracking for one conversation |
| clips | 1,027,960 | 30 seconds of one recording |
| moments of interest | 16,256 | a moment the participant or an outside annotator marked as notable (Meta's labels) |

- **Coverage.** 128,705 recordings (8,072 hours) were measured. The other 665 have
  no body-tracking data or are missing files, and their rows say which.
- **Expressivity.** A score from 0 to 1, plus a low/medium/high label. It combines
  four measurements:
  - how fast the arms move;
  - how far the arms move;
  - how much the head moves;
  - how much the fingers move.

  Facial movement (which Meta provides only for V00) and variation in voice
  loudness are separate scores, not mixed into the main one.
- **Posture.** Each clip and each recording gets one of five labels, with a
  confidence:
  - standing;
  - sitting;
  - mixed: both, each held for a sustained stretch;
  - unclear: the body is visible but the measurement is borderline;
  - unknown: the legs aren't visible.

  Each conversation also records both partners' postures together, for example one
  standing and one sitting.
- **Conversation.**
  - Whether both partners' recordings exist and line up in time.
  - How much the partners talk over each other, how evenly they share the talking,
    and how often the speaker changes.
  - Both partners' postures and expressivity.
  - Their relationship.
  - The prompt's type and Meta's interpersonal-style codes for it (IPC).
  - Flags for people who appear in more than one of Meta's train/dev/test splits.
- **Also, for each clip:**
  - arm, hand and head movement;
  - whether the person is speaking or listening;
  - how much of the body is in frame, and which way the person faces;
  - how reliable the body tracking is;
  - audio quality, and how much of the partner's voice leaks into the microphone;
  - counts of Meta's moments of interest;
  - image sharpness, and whether the camera moved.
- **Missing values** mean "not known" and are never filled in as 0. For example, a
  clip from a recording Meta never annotated for moments of interest has a blank
  count, not 0. Each column's documentation says when it can be blank and where the
  value came from:
  - Meta's data as released;
  - calculated from Meta's data;
  - carried over from our earlier pipeline;
  - newly computed.

## 2. How well it holds up

- **Posture.**
  - We have sitting/standing hand labels for 66 V00 recordings. The posture
    labeller made a call on 43 of them and got 41 right. On the other 23 it said
    "unclear" rather than guessing.
  - I also checked clips by eye: "standing" was right on 48 of 48 clips, and
    "sitting" on 23 of 24 (V00 and V03).
- **Expressivity.**
  - People score much higher while speaking (about 0.70) than while listening
    (about 0.25), in every vendor.
  - On V00, where Meta provides facial features, the score rises with facial
    movement (rank correlation 0.38). That's an independent signal pointing the
    same way.
  - It does not line up with Meta's moments of interest. Clips that contain one are
    no more expressive than other clips from the same recordings (AUC about 0.5,
    i.e. chance). See question 1.
- **Carried-over measurements.** 22 measurements reused from our earlier pipeline
  give exactly the same values as before on about 500,000 clips.
- **Consistency.** Automated checks pass:
  - every clip links to its recording and to its partner's clip;
  - clips cover each recording with no gaps or overlaps;
  - every value is within its allowed range.

  I also re-read 60 random clips straight from Meta's files, and they matched the
  tables.

## 3. Judgment calls you may want to change

1. **Clip length and alignment.** Clips are 30 seconds long and start at 0, 30, 60
   seconds and so on in both partners' recordings. So the same clip number covers
   the same stretch of time for both people. The length is a single setting, but
   changing it means re-running the measurement step over the whole dataset.
2. **What expressivity measures, and what it's compared against.**
   - It is based on body movement only.
   - A score of 0.7 means the person moves more than in 70% of clips from the same
     vendor's camera setup.
   - I compare within each setup because the body tracking differs between
     vendors. At the same amount of speaking, V02's tracked movement reads 30–40%
     faster than the others'. Ranked across the whole dataset, the score would
     mostly reflect which vendor recorded the clip.
   - A whole-dataset version is included for anyone who wants it.
3. **For posture, "unclear" is preferred over guessing.**
   - Borderline measurements are labelled "unclear".
   - For V01 and V02 the posture labeller never says "sitting". We have no hand
     labels for those vendors, and every "sitting" call I checked by eye there was
     in fact a standing person.
   - Some V01 videos are stored horizontally stretched, and Meta's 3D body fits for
     them are distorted as a result. Posture is "unknown" for those clips (about
     44% of V01).
   - **Perching counts as standing.** By perching I mean half-sitting on a tall
     stool or the edge of a table, with the legs nearly straight and the feet on
     the floor. Posture is read from the hip and knee angles, and a perched person
     has the angles of someone standing.
4. **When Meta's speech timestamps stop early.** Meta provides timestamps for when
   each person is speaking. In about 13,000 clips these stop partway through, while
   the transcript shows the person still talking. Speech-based values for those
   clips are left blank instead of being treated as silence.
5. **Meta's train/dev/test splits are kept as released.**
   - Meta split by participant, but the split isn't perfectly clean. 26 people
     appear in two splits, and about a quarter of dev conversations include someone
     who also has training data.
   - I flag these cases rather than making new splits, so results stay comparable
     with anyone else using Meta's splits.
6. **No single "good data" score.** What counts as good data depends on the model
   being trained. The tables give the ingredients and leave the choice to the user.

## 4. Left out on purpose

- **Each participant's IPC codes.** Meta gives codes for the two roles in a prompt
  (A and B) but doesn't say which participant played which role.
- **Facial features for V01–V03.** Meta released them only for V00.
- **Number of people in view.** Each file tracks one person, and the frames I
  sampled show only that person.
- **Pitch and intonation.** These would need a new audio analysis.
- **Topic beyond the prompt itself, and the order of conversations within a
  session.** Neither is recorded in the release.

## 5. Questions

1. **Expressivity.** Is body movement the meaning of "expressive" you want? Meta's
   moments of interest mark changes in how a person feels or what they are
   thinking, and most of them don't show up as movement. So I kept them as their
   own column rather than part of the score. If you had something broader in mind,
   such as including face, voice or moments of interest, tell me and I'll rework
   the score.
2. **Posture.**
   - Is it worth a small labelling round to check posture clip by clip? I'm thinking
     of a few hundred clips per vendor, each labelled standing, sitting, perched or
     mixed. Right now the evidence is the 66 recording-level hand labels plus my
     visual check.
   - For your purposes, should perching count as standing or as sitting?
